# Week 01 — three more official cases

Research for the Fall 2026 *Contents Programming* orientation (first-year art/design, Korea). Students already read works as **INPUT / RULE / OUTPUT**.

**Out of bounds (already on week 01 OT).** Vera Molnár, *Interruptions*; Ryoji Ikeda, *datamatics* / *data.tron*; Aaron Koblin, *Flight Patterns*; Fernanda Viégas & Martin Wattenberg, *Wind Map*; Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Room*.

**Out of bounds (later weeks).** Casey Reas, *Process* (week 5); Giorgia Lupi & Stefanie Posavec, *Dear Data* (week 9); W. E. B. Du Bois 1900 charts (week 11); Sol LeWitt, *Untitled from Squares with a Different Line Direction in Each Half Square* (week 4).

**Gaps these three fill.**

1. Readable data visualization: values, scale, legend, and encodings printed on the object.
2. Generative / instruction work that does not need a computer.
3. Data art whose medium is language, voice, or a physical object — not a map, not heartbeat lights, not an Ikeda-style data wall.

**Method.** Claims below are taken from artist sites, museum collection and exhibition pages, Gallica/BnF records, and the original objects. Wikipedia and blogs are not used as evidence. URLs were live-checked by GET on 31 August 2026 (browser user-agent). HTTP 200 unless noted.

**Classroom image rule (same as `week-01-famous-cases.md`).** Do not copy copyrighted photographs onto the course site. Open official pages in class. Public-domain plates may be hosted with institutional credit.

## 1. Charles-Joseph Minard, *Carte figurative… campagne de Russie 1812–13*, 1869

**Artist / title / year.** Charles-Joseph Minard (1781–1870), *Carte figurative des pertes successives en hommes de l’armée française dans la Campagne de Russie 1812–13 (comparées à celle d’Hannibal durant la 2ème Guerre Punique)*, Paris, 20 November 1869. Lithograph: two figurative maps on one coloured sheet, 44.5 × 52 cm on a 49 × 56.5 cm sheet. Bibliothèque nationale de France, département Cartes et plans, GE DON-4182 ([Gallica record](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x); [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb40650878p); [Gallica OAI](https://gallica.bnf.fr/services/OAIRecord?ark=ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)). Lithography credit on the plate: Régnier & Dourdet ([same Gallica object](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)).

**Official URLs (open in class).** All 200 on 31 August 2026.

- Original zoomable sheet (use this): [https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)
- Same object, item viewer: [https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x/f1.item](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x/f1.item)
- BnF catalogue notice: [https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb40650878p](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb40650878p)
- Machine-readable rights: [https://gallica.bnf.fr/services/OAIRecord?ark=ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x](https://gallica.bnf.fr/services/OAIRecord?ark=ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)

**INPUT / RULE / OUTPUT.**

- **INPUT.** Historical troop counts, the route into and out of Russia, and (on the retreat) temperature, compiled from named sources printed on the sheet: “les ouvrages de MM. Thiers, de Ségur, de Fezensac, de Chambray et le journal inédit de Jacob, pharmacien de l’Armée depuis le 28 Octobre” ([Gallica plate](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)).
- **RULE.** Also printed on the work: “Les nombres d’hommes présents sont représentés par les largeurs des zones colorées à raison d’un millimètre pour dix mille hommes; ils sont de plus écrits en travers des zones. Le rouge désigne les hommes qui entrent en Russie, le noir ceux qui en sortent.” Under the retreat, a “TABLEAU GRAPHIQUE de la température en degrés du thermomètre de Réaumur au dessous de zéro” aligns cold to place and date ([same plate](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)).
- **OUTPUT.** A single flow map on which a tan/red band of advance collapses into a black band of return, with headcounts written on the band and a temperature line below. No software is required to read the mapping. The same sheet also carries a smaller Hannibal comparison map above; teach the lower Russia map first ([BnF catalogue: two maps on one sheet](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb40650878p)).

**Classroom sentence (not the same slot as Molnár, Ikeda, Flight, Wind, Pulse).** Molnár invents a line-grid and interrupts it; Ikeda, Flight, Wind, and Pulse turn live or sensed streams into atmosphere. Minard prints a scale, a colour key, source names, and a temperature axis on the object, so a first-year can recover values from the picture.

**Image policy.** Gallica OAI marks the record `dc:rights` “domaine public” / “public domain” ([OAI](https://gallica.bnf.fr/services/OAIRecord?ark=ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)). Creator dates on the same record: Minard, Charles-Joseph (1781–1870). Hosting a scan *from Gallica*, with credit “Bibliothèque nationale de France, département Cartes et plans, GE DON-4182,” is the clean option. Prefer linking the Gallica viewer in class so students see the original object, including the Hannibal comparison on the same sheet.

**One risk for first-years.** Two maps share one sheet (Hannibal above, Russia below), the legend is in French, and the temperature graph uses Réaumur, not Celsius ([Gallica plate](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x)). Point to the lower map, read the millimetre sentence aloud, and convert one labelled cold point so the axis is not a mystery. Some students will file the sheet under history class; counter by treating the printed legend as the program.

## 2. François Morellet, *6 répartitions aléatoires de 4 carrés noirs et blancs d’après les chiffres pairs et impairs du nombre π*, 1958

**Artist / title / year.** François Morellet (1926–2016), *6 répartitions aléatoires de 4 carrés noirs et blancs d’après les chiffres pairs et impairs du nombre π*, 1958. Six independent square panels, oil on wood, 80 × 80 cm each. Centre Pompidou, Musée national d’art moderne, inventory AM 1982-16, purchased 1982. Reverse inscription: “Morellet 1958 / N° 58057” ([Centre Pompidou object, English](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR); [French](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)). On view at Centre Pompidou-Metz in *François Morellet. 100 pour cent*, 3 April–28 September 2026 ([Pompidou object](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR); [Pompidou-Metz exhibition](https://www.centrepompidou-metz.fr/en/programme/expositions/francois-morellet)).

**Why not Sol LeWitt, *Wall Drawing #289*.** The MASS MoCA instruction page is live (200 on 31 August 2026): [https://massmoca.org/event/walldrawing289/](https://massmoca.org/event/walldrawing289/). Week 4 already teaches a different LeWitt print as the official **INSTRUCTION / SYSTEM** case — *Untitled from Squares with a Different Line Direction in Each Half Square*, 1971 ([MoMA 99388, used in `week-04-period1.html`](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/99388)). Both LeWitts are grids of directional lines. *#289* would also sit next to Molnár’s interrupted line-grid on the same OT. That is too much same-artist, same-visual-family for first-years. Morellet keeps the “program without a computer” slot and changes the artist, the token (filled squares, not lines), and the random source (digits of π, not a drafter).

**Official URLs (open in class).** All 200 on 31 August 2026.

- Work + method (English): [https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)
- Same notice (French, identical analysis): [https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)
- 2026 retrospective, on view during the start of Fall 2026: [https://www.centrepompidou-metz.fr/en/programme/expositions/francois-morellet](https://www.centrepompidou-metz.fr/en/programme/expositions/francois-morellet)

**INPUT / RULE / OUTPUT.**

- **INPUT.** Six pre-gridded panels, each divided into four equal squares, and an external number stream: the even and odd digits of π. Centre Pompidou: from 1958 Morellet used chance “pour déterminer, par exemple, la distribution du noir et du blanc sur une surface préalablement quadrillée” ([Pompidou analysis, Arnauld Pierre, 2007 catalogue](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)).
- **RULE.** The title is the program. Pompidou: “chacun d’eux est divisé en quatre carrés égaux qui seront peints en noir ou en blanc selon que l’on aura attribué l’un ou l’autre de ces caractères à la suite des chiffres pairs et impairs du nombre Pi.” “Le titre de l’œuvre se veut totalement transparent quant à la méthode compositionnelle retenue.” The series stops at six panels, “juste avant le retour de l’une ou l’autre de ces configurations” ([same page](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)).
- **OUTPUT.** Six 80 × 80 cm oil-on-wood panels whose black/white combinations vary (two black squares in the lower corners, one white on black in an upper corner, equal split, monochrome white, monochrome black). No computer, no plotter, no drafter’s taste: a human paints what the digits say ([same page](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)). The same Pompidou notice points to the later telephone-directory series *Répartition aléatoire de 40 000 carrés…* (1961) as the same even/odd rule at bitmap scale — a one-sentence scale-up, not a second case.

**Classroom sentence (not the same slot as Molnár, Ikeda, Flight, Wind, Pulse).** Molnár’s *Interruptions* is a computer-and-plotter drawing that randomly erases a line grid; Ikeda, Flight, Wind, and Pulse need sensors, feeds, or spectacle. Morellet’s rule runs on paper with the digits of π, and the output is six painted squares you can recount by hand.

**Image policy.** Object photographs: “© Adagp, Paris.” Photo credit: “Centre Pompidou, MNAM-CCI/Georges Meguerditchian/Dist. GrandPalaisRmn,” image ref. 4N82429 ([Pompidou credits](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)). Link the museum page. Do not host.

**One risk for first-years.** Six panels of four squares can look “too simple” until someone executes the even/odd rule on a 2×2 grid in thirty seconds. The title is long and in French; read it as a recipe, not as decoration. Do not let the 40,000-square series steal the ten minutes — it is the same rule, only denser ([Pompidou analysis](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR)).

**Verified LeWitt fallback (not recommended for this OT).** If the instructor wants the canonical Conceptual Art name instead: Sol LeWitt, *Wall Drawing #289*, July 1976, white crayon and black pencil grid on black wall. Instruction for the fourth wall at MASS MoCA: “A 6-inch (15 cm) grid covering each of the four black walls. White lines to points on the grids. Fourth wall: twenty-four lines from the center, twelve lines from the midpoint of each of the sides, twelve lines from each corner. (The length of the lines and their placement are determined by the drafter.)” ([MASS MoCA](https://massmoca.org/event/walldrawing289/)). Whitney accession 78.1.1–4; rights line “©Sol LeWitt/Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York” ([Whitney, 4th wall](https://whitney.org/collection/works/25530)). Paper analogue: [Whitney, “Drawing Instructions”](https://whitney.org/education/families/kids-art-challenge/sol-lewitt). Do not host installation photos.

## 3. Mark Hansen and Ben Rubin, *Listening Post*, 2001

**Artist / title / year.** Mark Hansen and Ben Rubin, *Listening Post*, 2001 (artist and Art Fund date) / 2005 (Science Museum Group object edition). Vacuum-fluorescent text displays, 8-channel audio, software, electronics. Artist page: 2001, “dimensions variable, vacuum-fluorescent tubes, 8-channel audio, software, electronics” ([EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)). Science Museum Group, London, object 2014-47: overall 3708.4 × 5918.2 × 787.4 mm; “Twenty one sets of eleven screen modules” (231 screens); eight speakers plus a subwoofer; installed at the Science Museum in 2008 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)). Whitney Museum exhibition: 17 December 2002–9 March 2003, curated by Debra Singer ([Whitney](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/listening-post)). Art Fund: live data, electronic components, computer software, copper wire, aluminium and loudspeakers; acquisition support 2007 ([Art Fund](https://www.artfund.org/our-purpose/art-funded-by-you/listening-post)).

**Official URLs (open in class).** All 200 on 31 August 2026.

- Artist statement + exhibition history (use this for INPUT / RULE): [https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)
- Science Museum collection (installation photographs, hardware inventory): [https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)
- Whitney exhibition record: [https://whitney.org/exhibitions/listening-post](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/listening-post)
- Art Fund acquisition note: [https://www.artfund.org/our-purpose/art-funded-by-you/listening-post](https://www.artfund.org/our-purpose/art-funded-by-you/listening-post)

**INPUT / RULE / OUTPUT.**

- **INPUT.** A live harvest of public internet language. The artists: “*Listening Post* culls text fragments in real time from thousands of unrestricted Internet chat rooms, bulletin boards and other public forums” ([EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)). Art Fund: “text fragments from Internet chat rooms… constantly updated” ([Art Fund](https://www.artfund.org/our-purpose/art-funded-by-you/listening-post)).
- **RULE.** Six movements, each a different arrangement of visual, aural, and musical elements, “each with it’s own data processing logic.” The fragments “are read (or sung) by a voice synthesizer, and simultaneously displayed across a suspended grid of more than two hundred small electronic screens” ([EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)).
- **OUTPUT.** A hanging curtain of 231 vacuum-fluorescent screens plus eight-channel sound and a subwoofer: language as choir, not as a chart or a map ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post); [EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)). Science Museum: “immerses the audience in computer-synthesised text, voice and other audio fragments sampled from the internet” ([SMG](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)).

**Classroom sentence (not the same slot as Molnár, Ikeda, Flight, Wind, Pulse).** Flight and Wind Map plot geography; Pulse Room maps heartbeats onto light bulbs; Ikeda floods a wall with abstract particles; Molnár does not use external records at all. *Listening Post* maps public language onto voice and readable text — you can hear the data and, for a moment, read it.

**Image policy.** Artwork rights on the Science Museum record: “EAR Studio”; credit “(c) Mark Hansen and Ben Rubin, 2005” ([SMG details](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)). Art Fund photograph line: “© Mark Hansen and Ben Rubin, all rights reserved” ([Art Fund](https://www.artfund.org/our-purpose/art-funded-by-you/listening-post)). Science Museum installation photographs on that page are released **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, with a parallel line “© The Board of Trustees of the Science Museum” ([SMG](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)). Still: link the collection page; do not rehost on the course site. Catalogue data in title/maker fields is CC0; the descriptive text is CC BY 4.0 ([SMG “Using our data”](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8091687/listening-post)).

**One risk for first-years.** The source is “unrestricted” public forums ([EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post)). Open artist and museum documentation, not a live chat scrape. 2001 chat rooms need one sentence of historical framing. The hanging grid of glowing modules can look like Pulse Room until you name the difference: no body sensor, the material is other people’s sentences.

## How to use the three in about 20 minutes

1. **Minard (7 min).** Open [Gallica GE DON-4182](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b52504201x). Ignore the upper Hannibal map. Read the millimetre sentence. Point: width = men, red = in, black = out, lower graph = cold. Ask what is *not* pictured (faces, flags, Napoleon).
2. **Morellet (7 min).** Open [Pompidou AM 1982-16](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/ckn5BR). Read the title as a recipe. Execute a 2×2 even/odd of π on paper (digit 3 odd, 1 odd, 4 even, 1 odd). Name INPUT (grid + π), RULE (even/odd → black/white), OUTPUT (this panel). One sentence: the computer is optional; the rule is not.
3. **Listening Post (6 min).** Open [EAR Studio](https://www.earstudio.com/projects/project-page/listening-post), then one SMG installation still. Name INPUT (public chat language), RULE (six movements, each with its own logic), OUTPUT (voice + 231 screens). Contrast with Pulse Room in one line.

Closing line: Minard maps facts onto a readable picture; Morellet generates form from a written rule; Hansen and Rubin give data a voice. Python is one runtime among others.
